# Deliver projects without confusing a prototype with production

Use this guide when starting a project, MVP, client engagement, or substantial prototype.
It helps select a route, test uncertainty, deliver approved scope, and assign operational ownership.
Substantial prototypes include multiple workflows, persistent data, integrations, or deployment.
Small disposable experiments use [prototype selection](/home/.agents/skills/prototype-selection/SKILL.md)
and the [session workflow](/docs/ai-harness/session-workflow.md) instead.
These recommendations are **proposals**, not a contract or universal standard.
A project owner decides product priorities; a delivery owner plans and verifies the work.

## Choose your route

- [Qualify, discover, decide, and deliver](lifecycle.md) for phase
  actions and hypothetical examples.
- [Price, contract, accept, and change scope](commercial.md) for client or paid delivery only.
- [Prototype and develop with AI](engineering.md) when testing
  uncertain workflows or building approved scope.
- [Release, support, and exit](operations.md) before connecting real
  users or assuming ongoing operational responsibility.
- [Research and limitations](/docs/spikes/ai-assisted-client-delivery.md) when evaluating
  the evidence behind these recommendations.

**Core** means normally necessary within an applicable project phase.
**Conditional** means a trigger must justify its cost.
The linked guides state when to skip conditional activities and what follows.
These are decision aids, not a mandatory document factory.

## Operating agreement

At each applicable phase, name the project owner, delivery owner, and eventual operator.
For client work, the project owner is the authorized client sponsor.
One person may fill multiple roles; client acceptance still belongs to the authorized representative.
Record the next decision, evidence, responsible person, and deadline proposal when useful.
Stop when value, feasibility, authorization, or safe operation cannot be established.

Tailor depth to risk, not organization size. A small configuration change may
need one workflow and a receipt. Money movement, personal data, offline sales,
or multi-location stock demand stronger reconciliation and recovery evidence.
Disposable prototypes need bounded observations, not production hardening.
Non-client unpaid prototypes need no invoicing or client-contract workflow.
Client or paid prototypes still need applicable legal and commercial terms.
Discovery determines which risks apply; this guide does not presume a production stack.

**Portable boundary:** Keep project facts and open questions in their owning workspace, not this guide.
A roadmap is not automatically an endorsed product specification.
