# Set commercial terms for client or paid delivery

Use this page only for client or paid delivery.
For phase timing, start at the [project-delivery overview](README.md) or [lifecycle](lifecycle.md).
Non-client work without payment has no invoicing or client-contract workflow.
Client prototypes may still need confidentiality, intellectual-property, or other legal terms.
Assess those obligations before sharing materials or starting work.

These are **recommended negotiating defaults**, not legal advice. Obtain
jurisdiction-specific legal, tax, fiscal, and privacy review before relying on
contract language. No percentage, duration, price, or client obligation below is
agreed.

## Before work starts

**Core when selling discovery:** Offer paid discovery as a fixed deliverable and price,
independent of implementation. Example scope: observe receiving and checkout, compare vendor
fit, and deliver a route recommendation. **Core:** Name client sponsor,
consultant, access obligations, exclusions, assumptions, delivery window, and
payment currency. **Conditional:** Use time-and-materials for uncertain
integration investigations. Skip if work is sufficiently bounded; consequence:
fixed-price changes require renegotiation. **Conditional:** Set an expense or
AI-usage cap when external services incur variable costs. Skip when negligible;
consequence: client accepts documented spending uncertainty.

A proposal should list outcomes, journeys, acceptance examples, deliverables,
dependencies, fees, invoice events, and expiry. List optional outcomes
separately with price ranges only after estimation. Example exclusion:
payment-terminal integration needs separate vendor access and approval.

## Payment and acceptance

**Core when invoicing:** Agree invoice triggers, due dates, late-payment response, tax
treatment, and permitted pause before starting. Suggested default: discovery
payable at booking; implementation invoiced against approved, observable
milestones. Avoid charging by lines of code, AI usage, or claims of speed.
**Core:** Define a proposed review window, authorized approver, defect-report
channel, and objective acceptance examples. Example: Sponsor reviews a synthetic
transfer walkthrough and reports mismatches within the agreed window.
Silence-based acceptance requires explicit contractual and legal review; never
presume it.

**Conditional:** Hold a final balance until operational handover when release is
included. Skip if handover is outside scope; consequence: final payment follows
the agreed earlier milestone. **Conditional:** Use a deposit when capacity
reservation or external purchases create exposure. Skip when exposure is
negligible; consequence: consultant carries that scheduling risk.

## Control changes without blocking learning

**Core:** Record each change's requested outcome, impact on cost, schedule,
acceptance, and support. The sponsor approves or declines a revised offer before
added work starts. Example: Split tender moves from a demo question into priced
production scope only after payment-provider feasibility. Defects against agreed
acceptance stay inside the original scope. New journeys, changed policies, or
previously unresolved requirements require an impact decision. Pause dependent
work when a new requirement alters regulated behavior or security risk.

## Ownership and exit

**Core:** Negotiate intellectual-property assignment or license timing,
pre-existing materials, third-party licenses, and data ownership. Recommended
default: client owns its data and controls business accounts; consultant retains
clearly identified reusable methods. Contract counsel must resolve code
ownership, payment conditions, and permitted reuse. **Core:** Specify export
format, credential transfer, retention and deletion periods, transition
assistance, and paid support boundaries. **Conditional:** Add escrow or
source-transfer commitments for material lock-in. Skip when client-controlled
repositories and exports suffice; consequence: replacement may depend on
consultant availability. **Conditional:** Define service credits or response
guarantees when uptime is contractually essential. Skip without measured support
capacity; consequence: use best-effort terms with explicit coverage limits.

**Practical proposal card:** Outcome: reduce manual stock-transfer
reconciliation. Deliverable: demonstrated receiving journey and discrepancy
report. Exclusions: tax issuance and card settlement. Evidence: sponsor
validates three synthetic scenarios. Commercial fields: fees, dates, acceptance
window, payment events, ownership, support, exit. All values await negotiation.
